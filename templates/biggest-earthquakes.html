The Giants: Top 10 Strongest Earthquakes in Recorded History

📅 Updated: May 2026 ⏱️ 12 min read 🖊️ By RAA Historical Research

Since the invention of modern seismometers in the early 20th century, we have been able to objectively measure the massive release of energy during "great" earthquakes. This historical record not only serves as a ledger of past tragedies but also as a critical tool for understanding the ultimate potential of the Earth's tectonic plates. Here, we analyze the top 10 most powerful earthquakes recorded since 1900, ordered by their Moment Magnitude (MW).

Data Note: These rankings are based on Magnitude (energy released), not necessarily on casualties or property damage. Impact depends heavily on depth and proximity to populated areas.

1. Valdivia Earthquake, Chile (1960) — Magnitude 9.5

The 1960 Valdivia earthquake is the most powerful seismic event ever recorded. Occurring under the Nazca Plate's subduction zone off the coast of Chile, the rupture lasted for about 10 minutes. The resulting tsunamis affected the entire Pacific basin, with waves reaching up to 25 meters in Chile and crossing the ocean to cause deaths in Japan, Hawaii, and the Philippines.

2. Great Alaska Earthquake (1964) — Magnitude 9.2

Also known as the Good Friday Earthquake, it struck the Prince William Sound region of Alaska. It was the most powerful earthquake in North American history. The shaking lasted nearly 4.5 minutes and triggered numerous landslides and a massive tsunami that devastated several Alaskan towns and struck the west coast of the US and Canada.

3. Sumatra-Andaman Islands Earthquake (2004) — Magnitude 9.1

This earthquake, occurring off the west coast of northern Sumatra, triggered one of the deadliest tsunamis in human history. The undersea rupture caused the entire planet to vibrate as much as 1 centimeter. The resulting tsunami struck 14 countries bordering the Indian Ocean, claiming over 230,000 lives and bringing global attention to the need for tsunami early warning systems.

4. Tōhoku Earthquake, Japan (2011) — Magnitude 9.1

Striking off the eastern coast of Japan, this undersea megathrust earthquake was the most powerful ever to hit Japan. It moved the main island of Honshu 2.4 meters to the east and shifted the Earth on its axis by estimated 10 to 25 centimeters. The catastrophic tsunami it triggered led to the Fukushima Daiichi nuclear disaster.

5. Kamchatka Earthquake, Russia (1952) — Magnitude 9.0

Occurring off the Kamchatka Peninsula in the Russian Far East, this earthquake triggered a major tsunami that reached Hawaii, causing significant property damage but no fatalities. It remains the largest earthquake in Russian history.

6. Maule Earthquake, Chile (2010) — Magnitude 8.8

Striking off the coast of central Chile, this earthquake occurred at the boundary between the Nazca and South American tectonic plates. It was felt in much of Chile and parts of Argentina. The event triggered a tsunami that devastated several coastal towns and was detected as far away as San Diego, California.

7. Ecuador-Colombia Earthquake (1906) — Magnitude 8.8

This powerful earthquake occurred in the Pacific Ocean off the coast of Ecuador and Colombia. It produced a large tsunami that caused hundreds of deaths along the coast of Colombia. Due to the era, scientific data is less granular than more recent events, but its seismic moment remains among the top 10.

8. Rat Islands Earthquake, Alaska (1965) — Magnitude 8.7

Occurring in the Aleutian Islands, this earthquake triggered a tsunami that was reported to be 10 meters high on Shemya Island. Because of its remote location, damage and casualties were minimal, but its scientific significance as a major subduction event is substantial.

9. Assam-Tibet Earthquake (1950) — Magnitude 8.6

Unique among this list, the 1950 Assam-Tibet earthquake was a continental earthquake (not undersea). It was the result of the collision between the Indian and Eurasian plates. It caused massive landslides that blocked rivers and led to catastrophic flooding in the Brahmaputra valley. It remains the most powerful earthquake ever recorded on land.

10. Nias Earthquake, Indonesia (2005) — Magnitude 8.6

Occurring just months after the 2004 Sumatra tsunami, this earthquake struck off the coast of Sumatra. The event devastated the island of Nias and was felt as far away as Thailand and Malaysia. It is a stark reminder of how one major seismic event can trigger others by shifting stress along a fault line.

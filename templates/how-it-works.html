Architecture & Transparency

How RAA Earthquake Works

A transparent look at our data pipelines, seismic ingestion workflows, mapping architecture, and editorial methodology.

Platform Overview: RAA Earthquake is an independent data aggregation, mapping, and educational platform. We transform publicly accessible open-science earthquake feeds into accessible visual dashboards, searchable catalogs, and life-safety educational content.

1. Real-Time Data Pipeline

Seismic events occurring around the globe are recorded by thousands of seismometers operated by global geological networks. RAA Earthquake connects directly to the United States Geological Survey (USGS) Earthquake Hazards Program via their standardized RESTful GeoJSON API feeds.

🛰️ Feed Ingestion

We fetch real-time GeoJSON summaries across multiple temporal resolutions: past hour (all magnitudes), past 24 hours, past 7 days, and past 30 days (M4.5+).

⚡ Telemetry Parsing

Each event object is extracted with its primary attributes: Moment Magnitude (Mw/Mb), 3D spatial coordinates (Latitude, Longitude, Hypocentral Depth), timestamp, felt reports, and tsunami alert flags.

🗺️ Geospatial Rendering

Data points are projected onto high-performance Leaflet.js interactive maps with dynamic color scaling and proportional circular marker geometries.

2. Magnitude Color Coding & Classification

To help users instantly understand the severity of an event at a glance, RAA Earthquake applies a standardized visual hierarchy based on moment magnitude:

Magnitude Tier Visual Badge Typical Energy & Ground Effect
Minor / Light (< 4.0) < 4.0 Often felt indoors by few people; rarely causes damage.
Moderate (4.0 – 5.9) 4.0–5.9 Felt by everyone; slight damage to poorly constructed buildings near epicenter.
Strong (6.0 – 6.9) 6.0–6.9 Can cause significant structural damage in populated areas up to 100 km across.
Major / Great (7.0+) 7.0+ Severe damage over large regional areas; potential trigger for regional tsunamis if subduction-related.

3. Client-Side Alert System

Our platform includes a browser-based monitoring routine that periodically checks for major new seismic detections (≥ M6.0). When users opt-in via standard browser notification permissions:

  • The browser client polls the USGS 1-hour real-time feed once every 60 seconds.
  • If a new high-magnitude earthquake ID is identified that was not previously displayed, a notification banner is displayed with the location and magnitude.
  • No private user data or geolocation is transmitted or stored on external servers to power this feature.

4. Technical & Data Limitations

Transparency is fundamental to building trust. Users should be aware of the following technical characteristics of seismic monitoring:

  • Preliminary Determinations: Initial data published within 5–15 minutes of an earthquake is often automatically computed by computer algorithms. Seismologists subsequently review the waveform data manually, which may lead to revisions in magnitude, exact epicenter coordinates, or depth.
  • Sensor Density Variations: Regions with dense seismometer arrays (e.g., California, Japan, Europe) detect micro-earthquakes down to M1.0 or lower. Remote oceanic zones or regions with sparse telemetry may only reliably report events above M4.0.
  • Not an Early Warning Broadcast: RAA Earthquake is an informational web service and does not function as a substitute for government-run Earthquake Early Warning (EEW) systems (such as ShakeAlert in the US, JMA EEW in Japan, or National Disaster Emergency Broadcasts in India).

5. Open Data & Attribution

RAA Earthquake respects open-science data licenses and attributes all seismic telemetry to the United States Geological Survey (USGS) and partner regional networks. We also reference hazard mapping methodologies developed by the National Disaster Management Authority (NDMA) of India, IMD, and international geophysical organizations.
